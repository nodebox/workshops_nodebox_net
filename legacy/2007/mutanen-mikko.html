successful memes might prove detrimental to their hosts

I see many memes as being harmful and distracting, taking the victim further away from nirvana. Zen meditation, in the form of a soft audiovisual projection, can act as a countermeme: a white spell to banish rampant, distracting memes, that could be harmful to the host. This work aims to keep the mind clear from memollution (meme pollution). Keep your city clean!

WARNING! Like traditional drugs, my version of antimemetics also has side effects: irritation, drowsiness and anxiety.

Background

Must a meme-eating machine necessarily be chaotic / undefinable / transmuting? Because it must not be a meme in itself: otherwise, a meme is only replaced, and no space is freed in the mind. What kind of an attack of signals, of words, symbols and meaningless noise could have a strong core, strong enough to be more powerful than a meme, to override a meme? It cannot be expected, that all memes would succumb to one type of countermeme: the medication needs to be administered and prescribed, the dosage needs to be right: and the treatment’s duration appropriate.

1. the city

The font: a building grows based on every character typed. Buildings in the background are hidden by the buildings in front, in a basic ortographic grid of 4 x 5. The city floats in space in light pastel colours : a soft, soothing view with no harsh detail.

a soft analogy :: in medicine

Do we have a responsibility to stop disease? When we see another person, succumbing to the illness … do we have a responsibility to stop harmful memes from spreading? What if preventing disease/memes actually interferes with natural evolution? What do we want, as a species?
